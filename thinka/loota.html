Loota — a real-world
skill treasure hunt.

Loota turns a city into a game board. A business sponsors real prizes and puts a screen in the wild; anyone can scan the QR code and play for free. How the game works

The full game loop.

1
Discover a hunt & scan the QR
A sponsoring business displays a screen — "Win [prize] · [N] winners." Because the sponsor has already funded the prizes, the offer is real, and the business itself confirms it in person. Anyone nearby can scan and join.
2
Everyone starts equal — free to enter
There is no entry fee and no stake. Every player begins with the same conditions. This is a skill competition, not a lottery — you can win without spending a naira.
3
Navigate the map — speed is a choice, not a shortcut to winning
Players travel to quiz locations. You can walk (free) or optionally rent in-game speed tools — a bicycle, motorbike or car — to move faster. Speed alone never wins: you still have to solve the challenge when you arrive, so a sharp walker can beat a hurried driver.
4
Solve challenges — collect keys
Each location has a quiz or puzzle. Solve it, collect a key. The players who complete the challenge set fastest and correctly are the winners — the number of winners is set by the sponsor's campaign.
5
Winners claim a pre-funded prize
Winners collect their prize from the sponsoring business, which already holds it because the campaign was pre-paid. Verification happens through the platform. Players are never paid cash and cannot withdraw money from Loota.
Important

Loota is a skill game — not gambling.

Players do not stake or wager money, there is no entry fee, and outcomes are not decided by chance or a random draw. Winners are determined purely by skill (solving challenges) and strategy (route decisions), the same way a quiz or sports competition works. Prizes are funded in advance by sponsoring businesses. Because there is no stake and no cash payout to players, there is nothing to gamble with — the only payments on Loota are optional purchases of in-game items, described below.

Payments · Paystack

What users pay for, and how.

Everything a paying player can buy on Loota is an optional in-game item. Payments are processed by Paystack. Here are the exact questions a reviewer would ask, answered plainly.

Q1What specific products or services do users pay for through Paystack?
Users pay only for optional, virtual in-game items that affect how they move through a hunt — never for the ability to win. These are:
  • Speed tools — renting a bicycle, motorbike or car to travel faster between locations.
  • Travel consumables — fuel and rest, which keep a rented vehicle moving.
  • Hints & premium items — optional clues at a quiz location and premium speed items.

Typical prices are small (for example ₦50–₦2,000 per item). Loota is free to play; these purchases are convenience only and never guarantee a win, so many players never pay at all.
Separately, sponsoring businesses pay campaign/placement fees to run a hunt — a business-to-business arrangement, not a consumer purchase.

Q2How do these paid features work?
There are no subscriptions, no entry fees and no stakes on Loota — only one-off in-game purchases. While in an active hunt, a player can choose to buy an item (e.g. "rent a motorbike"). They pay through Paystack and the item is unlocked in their game immediately. Purchases are consumed within that hunt. Players do not top up a cash wallet and cannot withdraw money; prizes are physical goods provided by the sponsor, not cash.
Q3Where do users make payments? (the payment flow)
Payments happen inside the Loota app at the moment a player buys an item. The flow is three steps — choose an item, pay with Paystack (card, bank transfer or USSD), and the item unlocks. An illustration of the exact flow is below; the live flow can be seen at playloota.com.
Q4Is this Paystack account for Loota specifically, or for other Thinka products?
This Paystack account is operated by Thinka Platforms Ltd and is used to collect payments for Loota — specifically the optional in-game item purchases described above.
